This is the first post on the new site, so it seemed right to start with how I got here, as it isn't the usual route into software.

## Offshore

I started out in telecoms, and from 2005 to 2017 most of my working life was spent on oil and gas platforms, mainly in the North Sea. The early work was installing and commissioning control and shutdown systems, and later I looked after the communications on board: VSAT, PABX, PA/GA, radios, GMDSS and the Cisco network that tied them together, plus SCADA on some assets.

Offshore you can't phone a vendor and wait three days for someone to turn up. The fault is yours to fix, and the systems you're working on are often the ones that keep people safe. That changes how you approach a problem - you check your assumptions first, you don't push a change through because the clock says so, and you write down what you did so the next person on shift isn't guessing.

## Flying

Alongside that I was a flying instructor from 2001 to 2015. Teaching someone to fly is mostly teaching them to make decisions under pressure: stay calm, work the problem in the right order, and don't let a small issue grow because nobody made a call. I didn't realize at the time how much of that would carry over into leading engineers.

## AWS

In 2017 I joined AWS as a network technician in Osaka, then spent two and a half years as a program manager in data center operations across Japan and Asia-Pacific. From there I moved into engineering, first on modular data centers and edge hardware, then on identity infrastructure used across Amazon, and now on bot and fraud prevention for Amazon's hourly hiring.

The domains changed a lot, the job **didn't** really. It is still keeping systems running that people depend on, and being honest about what we actually know about them rather than what the design says.

## What's next

I plan to write here about the things I work on and think about: operational safety, incident response, bot prevention at the edge, and the move from engineer to leader. Some of it will be longer pieces and some short notes.
