This is the first post on the new site. It isn't the usual route into software, so I thought I'd start with how I got here.

## Offshore

I started out in telecoms. From 2005 to 2017 most of my working life was spent on oil and gas platforms, mainly in the North Sea. The early work was installing and commissioning control and shutdown systems. Later I looked after the communications on board: VSAT, PABX, PA/GA, radios, GMDSS and the Cisco network that tied them together. On some assets that included SCADA as well.

Offshore you can't phone a vendor and wait three days for someone to turn up. The fault is yours to fix. The systems you're working on are often the ones that keep people safe, and that changes how you approach a problem. You check your assumptions first. You don't push a change through because the clock says so. And you write down what you did, so the next person on shift isn't guessing.

## Flying

Alongside that I was a flying instructor in the south of France from 2001 to 2015, taking students from ab-initio to a full PPL. Teaching someone to fly is mostly teaching them to make decisions under pressure. Stay calm, work the problem in the right order, and don't let a small issue grow because nobody made a call. I didn't realize at the time how much of that would carry over into leading engineers.

## Amazon

I joined AWS in 2017 as a network technician in Osaka, Japan. After that I spent two and a half years as a program manager in data center operations across Asia-Pacific. Then I moved into engineering. First on modular data centers and edge hardware, then on identity infrastructure used across Amazon. Today I lead bot and fraud prevention for a high-traffic, customer-facing platform.

The domains changed a lot but the job **didn't** really. It is still keeping systems running that people depend on. And being honest about what we actually know about them, not what the design says.

## What's next

I plan to write about the things I work on, from operational safety to bot prevention and leading teams.
